# Design Review Checklist

&gt; This document defines the design quality bar for the Curling Performance Platform.

&gt;

&gt; Every new feature, screen, workflow and significant UI change should be reviewed against these criteria before implementation is considered complete.

&gt;

&gt; The purpose of this checklist is not perfection, but consistency. It ensures that the product continuously evolves towards a cohesive, premium athlete experience.

---

# Fundamental Principle

Every design decision should answer one question:

&gt; **Does this improve the athlete's ability to train, understand or improve?**

If the answer is unclear, the decision should be reconsidered.

---

# 1. Purpose

## Screen Purpose

- Does this screen have exactly one primary purpose?

- Is that purpose immediately obvious?

- Does every major element support that purpose?

- Has any functionality been added simply because there was available space?

---

## Primary Question

Can the screen clearly answer its primary question?

Examples:

- What should I do now?

- How do I start training?

- How am I performing?

- What did I learn?

If the screen answers multiple unrelated questions, it should be redesigned.

---

# 2. Information Architecture

## Information Priority

Can users identify within three seconds:

- the most important information?

- the primary action?

- the next logical step?

---

## Hierarchy

Does the screen contain:

- one Hero

- clear Primary content

- supporting Secondary information

- subtle Context

- understated Utility actions

Nothing should compete unnecessarily for attention.

---

## Progressive Disclosure

Are advanced controls hidden until they become useful?

Could a first-time athlete successfully use this screen without seeing every available option?

---

# 3. Visual Hierarchy

## Focus

Where does the eye go first?

Is that intentional?

If two unrelated areas compete equally for attention, hierarchy should be improved.

---

## Surfaces

Has every card earned its existence?

Could multiple cards become one?

Could a card become an inline section?

Does every surface communicate a different level of importance?

---

## Rhythm

Does the page alternate naturally between:

- open space

- grouped information

- emphasis

- lighter sections

Or is it simply:

Card

↓

Card

↓

Card

↓

Card

↓

Card

---

## Whitespace

Is whitespace improving understanding?

Or merely creating empty areas?

Conversely:

Would reducing whitespace actually improve the experience?

---

# 4. Typography

## Scanability

Can important information be found without reading paragraphs?

Are numbers easier to find than descriptions?

Do headings communicate structure?

---

## Emphasis

Is typography used before colour?

Would removing colour still preserve hierarchy?

---

# 5. Interaction

## Primary Action

Is the next action obvious?

Can the athlete complete the primary workflow without hesitation?

---

## Decision Making

Does the interface minimise unnecessary decisions?

Could defaults replace configuration?

Could guidance replace choice?

---

## Feedback

After every important action, does the interface reassure the user?

Examples:

- saved

- completed

- recorded

- exported

- restored

Feedback should be immediate and calm.

---

# 6. Cognitive Load

## Simplicity

Could anything be removed?

For every element ask:

If this disappeared tomorrow, would the athlete genuinely miss it?

If not,

remove it.

---

## Mental Effort

Is the athlete thinking about curling?

Or about operating the application?

The latter indicates poor design.

---

# 7. Performance Mindset

## Training First

Does this screen support training?

Or merely display information?

---

## Purposeful Analytics

If charts exist,

do they answer meaningful questions?

Not:

"What data do we have?"

Instead:

"What insight does this provide?"

---

## Improvement

Does this screen help the athlete improve?

Or simply observe?

Observation alone is rarely valuable.

---

# 8. Navigation

## Orientation

Can users always answer:

- Where am I?

- Why am I here?

- What happens next?

without explanation?

---

## Continuity

Does moving between screens feel natural?

Or does each page feel isolated?

Navigation should preserve mental flow.

---

# 9. Writing

## Clarity

Would an elite curler immediately understand the language?

Could any sentence become shorter?

---

## Tone

Does the interface sound:

- confident

- calm

- professional

- supportive

Never:

- robotic

- overly enthusiastic

- marketing driven

- playful

---

# 10. Premium Quality

Ask honestly:

Would this screen feel at home inside products like:

- WHOOP

- Linear

- Flighty

- Apple Fitness

- Oura

without looking copied?

---

Does the screen feel intentionally designed?

Or simply assembled?

---

Would this be a screenshot we would proudly use:

- in the App Store

- on the website

- during a product presentation

If not,

why not?

---

# 11. Curling Authenticity

Could this screen belong to any generic sports application?

If yes,

it probably isn't specific enough.

The platform should feel purpose-built for curling.

Terminology,

workflows,

measurements,

and interactions should reinforce that identity.

---

# 12. Consistency

Does this screen behave consistently with the rest of the application?

Would an experienced user correctly predict:

- layout

- navigation

- interactions

- terminology

- feedback

before using it?

Consistency builds trust.

---

# 13. Scalability

Will this design still work when the platform grows?

Consider future features such as:

- Coach

- Team

- Training Plans

- Cloud Sync

- Automatic Timing

- Athlete Profiles

Avoid solutions that solve today's problem while limiting tomorrow's product.

---

# 14. Accessibility

Does the design remain usable:

- on smaller phones?

- with larger text sizes?

- in poor lighting?

- during training on the ice?

Can all important information be understood without relying solely on colour?

Can interactive elements be comfortably reached with one hand?

---

# 15. Final Design Questions

Before approving any design, ask:

- Is this simpler than the previous version?

- Is this clearer than the previous version?

- Is this faster to understand?

- Does it reduce cognitive load?

- Does it support deliberate practice?

- Does it help athletes make better decisions?

- Does it strengthen the platform's visual identity?

- Would removing something improve the experience?

- Would I enjoy using this screen every day?

- Would a competitive curler trust this interface during training?

If multiple answers are "no", the design is not ready.

---

# Definition of Done

A design is considered complete only when it:

- has a single clear purpose

- communicates hierarchy instantly

- supports the athlete's workflow

- reduces cognitive load

- feels calm and professional

- aligns with the product identity

- strengthens the overall visual language

- integrates naturally with the existing information architecture

- scales to future features

- meets the quality standard defined in this checklist

The goal is not to create visually impressive interfaces.

The goal is to create software that athletes stop noticing because it feels so natural to use.
